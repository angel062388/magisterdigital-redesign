03 · How we cover a market

How a national agency runs a local market well

Most agencies that claim a city do it with a virtual office and a phone number that forwards somewhere else. We do not run that play. Magister Digital is one operator team headquartered in San Diego, and we run home service, medical, and legal marketing for businesses in the metros above as a remote partner, not a pretend local. The work that wins a market is not a local storefront. It is knowing how demand moves in that city, what the competitors are bidding, and which compliance rules govern the vertical, then running paid traffic, organic, automation, and conversion design against it under one team.

That is why each market page is built on real local context rather than a template with the city name swapped in. Phoenix home services lives and dies on the summer heat cycle and the Arizona Registrar of Contractors. Houston restoration follows hurricanes and floods. New York legal runs under some of the strictest bar advertising rules in the country. We build the strategy around what is actually true in your market, and we keep every claim inside what we can verify and defend.

The same three founders run every engagement regardless of city. Brian Hong leads strategy and AI operations. Michael Merlino owns local SEO and the automation stack. Dimitry Morgan owns paid media. Between them sits 50+ years combined SEO and paid media experience, and the same stack we run for clients runs an active construction company, TurnkeyRenovators, and draws on tooling built across Infintech Designs, CTR Geeks, and Denver Digital Agency.

04 · What stays the same in every market

The pricing does not move by zip code

Single-channel engagements start at $20,000 per month and full-stack engagements start at $60,000 per month in every market, on a 90-day initial term that then runs month-to-month. What changes between cities is the demand pattern, the competitive intensity, and the licensing and advertising rules for each vertical. Those differences shape the strategy, not the invoice.

We will not sell a market we cannot serve well, and we will not dress up a national engagement as a local one.
