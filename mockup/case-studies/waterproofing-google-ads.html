Money out every day, almost nothing back

The ads were live when the year started, and the spend was real. What came back was not.

04 · The diagnosis

The clicks were fine, the landing was not

01Not the budget, not the market

The problem was not the budget and it was not the market. Money was going out every day and clicks were landing; the account was buying attention and then wasting it.

02Where the clicks were landing

Clicks were landing on general website pages instead of pages built for the one thing the searcher wanted priced. When someone searches “basement waterproofing near me” and lands on a homepage, they leave.

03The fix that followed

The fix was where the click landed, not how much the click cost. Same searchers, same budget mechanics, different destination: a page about exactly the job they wanted priced.

05 · The build

A page for every service, a source on every call

What we did, in the order it mattered, plus the one thing only the client could do. Every move pointed at the same target: land the click on the job the searcher wanted priced, then count what happened next.

01Built a dedicated page per service

Basement waterproofing, French drains, crawlspace encapsulation, and window wells each got their own page, so every click landed on exactly what the searcher asked for.

02Put call tracking on every lead

Each call and form now carries its source, so budget decisions run on quote requests, not clicks.

03Steered budget to the big jobs

Spend concentrated on basement waterproofing and drainage, the jobs that carry the $8,500 average, instead of spreading evenly across everything.

04What the client changed

The owner runs his own estimates and answered the phone. Every request that came in got a fast response, and that follow-up speed is a real part of why requests became booked jobs.

05Did not do

Did not screen requests for spam or duplicates in these figures. Did not include the agency fee, so 6.9x is a media-only return. Did not project revenue from averages: $287,532 is the client's own booked-job records, confirmed in writing before publication.

08 · What went sideways

Two months missed, then a full calendar

Every case study has a sideways column. This one starts with a failing account and ends with a capacity problem.

  • 01January and February missed. Twelve quote requests across two months at up to $833 each is a failing account, and we ran it during the rebuild rather than pausing. From March 1 onward the account produced 87 requests in five months at $329 each
  • 02By August the owner's schedule was full three months out and he asked us to reduce spend. A capacity problem, which is the kind of problem this work is supposed to create

How the counting worked, and what it cannot see

  • 01A request is a Google Ads conversion: a tracked phone call or a completed quote form attributed by Google to an ad click. It is a person asking for a quote, not a booked job. Requests are not screened for spam or duplicates in these figures
  • 02Figures credit only leads Google ties to an ad interaction, which understates the ads' full effect: call-tracking data for the same account attributed additional leads to ad traffic that Google's own column does not count, and ads also influence people who later find the business through maps or search without clicking. None of that spillover is included here
  • 03The account's conversion-action definitions are pending a final verification pass
  • 04Cost basis: $41,102 is Google Ads media spend only, as reported by the platform. It does not include the agency fee, software, or production, so the 6.9x figure is a media-only return, not a return on total investment. The return is $287,532 divided by $41,102; cost per quote request is $41,102 divided by 99, or $415
  • 05Outside factors: waterproofing demand in the Northeast is seasonal and rises with spring rain; part of the second window's volume rides that season. Landing-page work and budget concentration happened in the same period, so the split between seasonal lift and account changes cannot be isolated from this data alone
  • 06Exhibit: a redacted Google Ads screenshot covering the full Jan–Jul window, client name and account ID barred, metrics visible, is pending and will be attached before publication

What this proves, and what it does not

The revenue figure is the client's own reported total of booked work, not an independently audited number, and booked work is not the same as collected payment. This is one account, in one market, at one budget level, with an owner who answers his own phone. A contractor with slower follow-up, a different market, or a different average ticket should not expect the same arithmetic.

Results are specific to this client's market, service, budget, capacity, and timeframe and are not a guarantee of similar outcomes.
