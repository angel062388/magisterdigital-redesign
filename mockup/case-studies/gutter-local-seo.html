04 · The diagnosis

Demand was never the problem, being the answer was

01What was broken

The reviews were coming in and the work was good, but the phone was quieter than the review count deserved. The website had pages for the towns they serve, and those pages were not showing up when a homeowner in those towns searched.

02What was actually wrong

The demand was already there. In their part of New Jersey, people search for gutter cleaning, repairs, guards, and drainage every week. The company just was not the answer that came up.

03And nobody was counting

When the company did come up, nobody was counting which channel deserved the credit. Without tracking, even a win is a rumor: the phone gets busier and no one can say why, which means no one can decide where the next dollar goes.

05 · The build

Make the map listing the front door

What we did, in the order it mattered, plus the one thing only the client could do. Most local buyers never reach a website; they call from the map. Everything else was built to back that up.

01Made the map listing the front door

Most local buyers never reach a website; they call from the map. The Google Business Profile got the ongoing attention a homepage usually gets, and it now produces 8 of every 10 leads.

02Rebuilt the town pages to rank

One generic page cannot win in twenty towns. Each service-area page was reworked to stand on its own for its town, backed by technical cleanup and a move to faster hosting.

03Tracked before claiming anything

Dedicated tracking numbers on the map listing and the website, plus form tracking, so every number on this page comes from a log, not a feeling.

04What the client changed

They answered the phone. Of 619 tracked calls in the window, 456 were picked up live. Marketing fills the line; the crew picking up is what turns a ring into a job.

05Did not do

Did not run ads: media spend for the whole window is $0. Did not publish a qualified-lead number the inconsistent tagging could not stand behind. Did not claim revenue, because booked-job data was not available to verify.

08 · What went sideways

Two honest notes, on the record

Every case study has a sideways column. This one has two entries, and they stay on the page.

  • 01487 is a raw count. Spam form bots and sales robocalls are in there, because lead qualification tagging was not applied consistently, and we will not publish a qualified number we cannot stand behind
  • 028 in 10 leads riding on one channel is a strength and a single point of failure. The town pages exist to spread that risk, and that work is still in progress

How the counting worked, and what it cannot see

  • 01A lead is any unique person who called a tracking number or submitted a website form. No qualification filter is applied, so the count includes some spam and solicitation contacts, and this page reports raw volume only and says so
  • 02Attribution comes from CallRail on a 50/50 model: dedicated tracking numbers on the Google Business Profile, a number pool on the website, and form tracking on the site
  • 03Known gaps: calls dialed to any untracked number are invisible, referrals and repeat customers who saved a direct number are invisible, and the Direct bucket of 41 leads likely contains people who found the company through search first. Attribution here is good, not perfect
  • 04Cost basis: $21,000 is the agency fee alone, $3,000 a month for January through July. No paid advertising ran, so media spend is $0, and cost per lead is $21,000 divided by 487, which is $43.12
  • 05Outside factors: gutter demand is seasonal and summer storms likely contributed to the July peak. Tracking began January 19, 2026, so there is no measured baseline from before that date, and the growth shown is growth within the tracked window

What this proves, and what it does not
